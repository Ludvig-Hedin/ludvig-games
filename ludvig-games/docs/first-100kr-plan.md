## The first 100 kr

Count real customer purchases, not free test orders, personal self-purchases, views or promised sales.

**Working milestone: two bundle purchases at 99 kr.** Customer spend: 198 kr. Estimated amount left
after example buyer VAT and provider/payout fees: about 134 kr. This is positive contribution before
our own operating costs and income tax, not necessarily profit and not bank cash yet.

The example uses a Swedish card buyer, price including 25% VAT, provider fee 6.5% + US$0.50 per order,
1% non-US bank payout fee and 30 October's illustrative USD/SEK rate 9.8264. Actual product tax treatment,
buyer location, payment method and settlement can differ. Formula and full table are in the
[reviewed research](ai-games-business-research-2026-10-30.md#52-what-ludvig-keeps-per-sale-illustrative).

Lemon Squeezy has a **US$50 minimum payout threshold** and a payout holding/schedule process.
First sales are not an immediate bank deposit. Under the same assumptions, eight bundle orders leave
roughly 537 kr after the illustrative payout fee, enough to clear US$50 at that FX if all are eligible
for payout and there are no refunds/other deductions. The dashboard determines actual payout timing.

### First sales experiment, after checkout is ready

1. Publish three approved clips per week for two weeks, two clips per game in total. Agent prepares and
   reports; only the owner or an explicitly authorized publisher posts. Start with the actual delight:
   a ridiculous drawing reveal, Pigeon chaos or a Karts drift. Show a paid item's appeal in context.
2. Use an honest free-play call to action and the actual game page. Customers can play before buying.
   Keep the bundle available without making it a requirement. No paid ads or unsolicited outreach.
3. Track clips published, meaningful site referrals, game/extras-page views, completed orders and
   recovery/refund/support issues. These are different steps, not one inferred conversion number.
4. If clips do not bring visits, improve the hook/distribution before changing price or making games.
   If visits do not become play, inspect the game's first minute. If interested players see the offer
   but do not buy, review what the pack adds. If checkout opens fail to become orders, inspect checkout.
5. Two weeks is a review point, not a promised earnings deadline. Little exposure means insufficient
   evidence, not proof the game is unwanted. Use qualified exposure and observed failures to decide
   the next small test, not an arbitrary calendar deadline.

## From sales to profit

Weekly report: customer spend, buyer tax, provider fees, refunds/chargebacks, money earned before
operating costs, payout status, hosting/tools/allocated agent costs, and owner/support time.
Do not count deferred payouts as a technical failure or contribution as profit.

**Cash operating profit = contribution after fees/refunds minus actual operating costs.** Track owner
time and a reasonable allocated share of existing agent subscriptions separately so 'free agents' do
not hide an uneconomic project. Sweden income tax and personal take-home remain separate.

Illustration using only 99 kr bundles, same fee assumptions, **300 kr monthly operating costs**:

- 2 orders: about 134 kr contribution; about 166 kr operating loss.
- 5 orders: about 336 kr contribution; about 36 kr operating profit.
- 10 orders: about 672 kr contribution; about 372 kr operating profit.
- 50 orders: about 3,359 kr contribution; about 3,059 kr operating profit.

The 300 kr is an example, not an approved budget. Use actual costs. No new subscriptions, ads or paid
APIs are authorized. Refunds and support can change these results. These are one-time purchases:
existing owners cannot buy the same entitlement every month. Sustained income requires new eligible
customers or useful separately approved new products, rather than counting the same audience again.

Keep the first month organic. After initial real orders, investigate requests for new places/content
before expanding the item catalog. Once a month, review whether contribution covers costs and whether
owner time is falling. Aim for two positive months as an operating checkpoint, not proof of a durable
business. Stop new content spending when qualified demand or margins do not justify it; repair clear
delivery bugs automatically. Preserve existing paid access and refund duties if expansion stops.

## Should we make another game?

**Recommendation: not before the first-sales experiment.** This does not cancel the approved lineup.
There are already enough games to test distribution and purchase intent. Building another one would
delay learning whether people pay and add another surface to maintain.

**Next product hypothesis, not approved:** a new Karts track pack, with one clearly previewed new
location. All current tracks stay free. Sell a new place to race, rather than changing kart performance.
Price/contents need a separate decision. The friend's story is a reason to test this, not a demand
forecast or permission to copy their game, art or maps.

If existing games cannot support that loop, a bounded new concept worth pitching is **RC Stunt Yard**:
drive a toy car, place ramps and record short tricks; one free yard, optional paid themed map packs.
Start with a prototype only, check whether the first minute makes clips people want to share, then
decide whether to build a paid game. No runtime AI requirement, subscriptions or surprise API bills.
It still needs its own scope approval and product validation; do not create it under this plan request.
